P.L.A.T.E. fundamentally changes what happens between pulling the trigger and staring at the post-raid screen.

Bullets behave like actual projectiles now, not just floating damage numbers. Armor protects you by physically stopping threats, rather than just acting like a second health bar. Wounds depend on exactly what the bullet passed through, and bleeding means your PMC is actually running out of blood—not taking psychic damage to his left leg every few seconds.

In short: Tarkov still Tarkovs you. Now physics just helps.

## Stash Tetriz & Loadouts

Your loadout matters before you even hit **READY**.

Don't take an MP5 loaded with cheap PST, push a Chad wearing a Gen4 with a rifle plate, and expect a frontal mag-dump to solve your problem. It might eventually, but you're going to have a very bad time finding out.

Pick ammo for the threats you actually expect to fight:

| Ammo Type | Performance Profile |
| --- | --- |
| **Flesh Damage (RIP, HP)** | Absolutely vaporizes an unarmored Timmy or a Scav, but utterly garbage against actual plates. |
| **Hardened AP** | Built to punch through plates, though the smaller projectile might leave a narrower wound channel. |
| **Heavy Subsonic** | Retains kinetic performance surprisingly well over distance. |
| **Light, Fast Rifle** | Devastating up close, but short barrels and long distances will quickly strip the magic out of them. |
| **Flechettes & Buckshot** | Flechettes punch deep. Buckshot makes a mess. Know the difference. |

Damage and pen numbers on the ammo charts are useful for comparison, but they aren't a binding contract signed by Nikita. A cartridge doesn't deal exactly “85 damage” every single time it touches meat.

Choose a weapon, barrel, and ammo combo that makes sense. Or just bring whatever Fence sold you and turn the raid into a peer-reviewed tragedy.

## In the Meat Grinder

Where you hit matters just as much as what you fired.

Clipping an arm isn't the same as sending a round through the center of someone's chest. "Center mass" isn't just a cope for people who can't consistently hit *(Head, Eyes)* anymore—it's where several vital organs are inconveniently stored.

- If you can't pen the plate, shoot around it (leg meta is still an option).
- If the face is exposed, the face gets clapped.
- A jaw hit is brutal, but it doesn't automatically mean the brain is destroyed.
- A round through the heart or spine doesn't give a damn about the target's remaining HP.
- A bullet stopped by bone might break it and dump all its residual energy into the limb.
- Rifle rounds can tumble and break apart if the meat they hit is deep enough.
- A bullet that exits an arm can continue straight into the torso behind it.

Range matters too. That short-barrel 5.56 that deletes someone across a dorm hallway on Customs is going to leave a much less impressive hole across the lumber yard on Woods.

Two hits from the exact same gun won't always make identical wounds. One tumbles early, the other stays point-first. One clips a rib, the other goes straight through a lung. Sometimes the guy drops instantly. Sometimes he runs away, bleeds out in a bush, and becomes a mystery corpse five minutes later.

## Armor Isn't Magic (Anymore)

Armor is no longer a percentage-based damage coupon. A plate has to physically stop the bullet.

- **Ceramic:** Incredible at stopping heavy rounds, but getting shot in the same spot twice means wearing it again is a death wish.
- **Steel:** Takes a beating and survives, but you'll still feel like Tagilla took a sledgehammer to your ribs.
- **Soft Armor:** Great for catching pistol rounds and shrapnel. It is not a hidden rifle plate just because the UI slapped an optimistic class number on it.

Buying zeroed or heavily damaged armor means you're just gambling that the next bullet won't find the exact spot where the last guy fucked up.

Even if the plate stops the round, Newton always gets his cut. You can still suffer pain, stamina drain, and contusions. Your helmet might catch a bullet, but you'll still be seeing stars and fighting a massive concussion.

## Cover Is a Thing Again

The wall between you and the guy shooting at you used to be a dice roll: his penetration number against a number the level designer typed onto the collider. Whatever won the roll came out the other side at full speed, damage untouched, as if the wall had been a rumour.

Now every obstacle is a material and a thickness, and going through it costs.

- **Doors are doors.** A wooden one is 50 mm of wood, an office steel one is a plate, a tin one is two skins over a frame. Bunker blast doors are still shut and will remain shut.
- **A drum is not a safe.** Barrels, canisters, container panels and car bodies are skin around air. You pay for the wall going in and the wall coming out, not for the six hundred millimetres of nothing in between.
- **Cars are cover, briefly.** A flank is 3 mm and you cross two of them. A pistol round dies inside the car. Rifle ball crosses the whole thing and finds whoever thought a Lada was a plate carrier.
- **The second wall costs more than the first.** A bullet leaving a barrier is tumbling, and a tumbling bullet hits the next one sideways. Rifle rounds that used to thread twelve sheets of tin now die on the sixth.
- **Ricochet belongs to the surface.** Concrete, wood, soil and water each have their own angle, and a surface can only bounce a round it could have stopped. Table tops have stopped mirroring P90 fire back at the man standing over them.

Shooting through cover works. It just isn't free, and neither is hiding behind it.

## Surviving the Aftermath

When the shooting stops, don't instantly go prone on the body to loot six mags of warm PRS. Find cover. Reload. Check if you're leaking.

Every serious wound bleeds, and severity depends on what got cut. Bleeding doesn't just tick down your limb HP—it drains a completely separate blood-volume resource. As your blood pressure drops, you get stamina loss, tremors, tunnel vision, and eventually, you lose the ability to sprint or jump. Keep ignoring it, and you'll just collapse and die while your limb bars still look surprisingly green.

And no, bleeding doesn't stop just because a hidden timer felt sorry for you. Fix it.

- **Bandages:** For light external leaks.
- **Tourniquets/Hemostatics:** For when you're painting the walls.
- **CMS / Surv12:** Can close wounds on the limb they repair (if enabled in settings).
- **Painkillers:** Keep you moving. They do not manufacture blood.

Blacked limbs cause massive, but treatable, external bleeding. Internal bleeding is a whole different nightmare. A destroyed abdomen, torn artery, or severe blunt trauma behind your armor can cause you to bleed internally where a tourniquet can't reach it. If your pressure keeps dropping and there's no visible wound left to patch, your raid just got a hard time limit.

Sometimes the big brain play isn't pushing "one more building," it's extracting before your circulatory system shuts down.

## Bleeding Out in a Bush

PMCs and Scavs play by the exact same medical rules you do.

An enemy retreating isn't necessarily flanking you. He might just be leaking a pint of tactical fluid behind a rock. You can trade shots, disengage, and find him dead later. But remember: you can also win the fight, grab his loot, and die 50 meters from extract because you forgot to check your own thorax.

Always confirm your own medical status before you confirm the kill.

## Blood Loss Follows You Home

Extracting doesn't magically top off your PMC's blood.

Lost blood carries over between raids and regenerates slowly. Queueing back up immediately while half-empty is a legitimate strategy, sure—in the same way that fighting Killa with a Makarov is a legitimate strategy.

Therapist sells Blood Transfusion Kits, and you can craft them at the Hideout's Medstation. Bring one. Carrying it costs space; not carrying it might cost everything else in your backpack.

## Shotguns & Grenades

Buckshot uses realistic pellet counts now (way more than the Tarkov-standard 8). Flechettes act like steel needles: they penetrate well but aren't great at ripping wide wounds. Less-lethal rounds won't act like they were built to hunt IFVs anymore.

Grenade shrapnel is dangerous way past the vanilla boundaries. Soft armor catches the small stuff, but chunky fragments will ruin your raid and your insurance return.

If you hear a nade bounce, get the hell out of the room.

## Adjusting the Suffering

Hit **F12**. You can tweak damage and bleeding separately for your PMC, bot PMCs, and Scavs. Keep it equally lethal for everyone, make bots less likely to bleed out in the woods, or crank all the sliders toward maximum *(Head, Physics)*.

Internal bleeding and several medical behaviors are also fully customizable.

## Compatibility & Uninstallation

P.L.A.T.E. completely rips out and replaces Tarkov’s ballistics, armor, and trauma systems. Other mods doing the same thing will break.

Modded ammo and armor from other creators are supported if they use physical properties. If another mod adds a weightless bullet traveling at Mach 10, P.L.A.T.E. will try to calculate it, and the results will be educational.

Fika support is currently untested.

### WARNING: UNINSTALLING

The only new item added to your actual profile is the Blood Transfusion Kit.

Before uninstalling P.L.A.T.E., you must delete every Transfusion Kit from your PMC's inventory and your stash. After that, removing the mod is safe.

Bring ammo that kills the armor you expect to fight.

Bring meds that fix the armor you expect to wear.

Stop trusting construction helmets.
